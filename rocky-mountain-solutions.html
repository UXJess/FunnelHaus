Rocky Mountain Solutions

Rocky Mountain Solutions is a Maple Ridge company covering construction safety, strata maintenance, and home renovations across the Lower Mainland. They needed a website that could explain three different jobs to three different buyers — a site superintendent, a strata council, and a homeowner — and give each of them a straightforward way to ask for the work. The goal was a professional site that made the company easy to trust and easy to hire.

Client
Rocky Mountain Solutions
Sector
Construction safety, strata, renovation
Location
Maple Ridge, BC
Scope
Design, build, conversion

Project overview

The company was already doing the work. What it lacked was a website that could carry any of it. Construction safety, strata care, and home renovations were competing for the same first impression, so a visitor could not tell who the company was for. A quote still depended on someone already knowing the phone number.

We designed and built rockymountainsolutions.ca from the ground up. The homepage opens on one line — your site, your strata, your home — then opens into three service pages, a coverage map for Maple Ridge, the Tri-Cities, and the Fraser Valley, and a request form that asks for the company, the service, the site, and the scope. A submitted request confirms that someone will be in touch within four business hours.

The company now has a site that explains the work and makes a quote straightforward. The phone number is still one tap away. A visitor can also read the service that matches the job, check the city, and send a request that already includes the scope.

We needed a site that actually brings in work, not just looks good. The FunnelHaus team built ours from the ground up and made it easy for people to request a quote. It was fast, fixed-price, and we'll be working with FunnelHaus moving forward.

Andrew B. Owner, Rocky Mountain Solutions

Approach

01

One line that sorts the visitor.

“Your Site. Your Strata. Your Home. Done Right.” Copper is reserved for the last two words. A superintendent, a strata manager, and a homeowner can each find themselves before they scroll, and the rest of the page only has to prove the line.

02

Three doors, one company.

Construction safety, strata maintenance, and home renovations each have a page. A WorkSafeBC conversation and a kitchen renovation need different proof. The homepage still holds the three together, so the business reads as one crew with one standard: do the work right, say what will happen, stand behind it.

03

A field palette, and the credentials up front.

Near-black, warm paper, and copper. The crest — mountains over a saw blade — sits large and quiet in the hero, so the page feels like the work. WorkSafeBC compliance, COR audit support, and OFA Level 3 sit beside the contact details. Those are the facts a contractor checks before they call.

04

Geography specific enough to hire against.

“The Lower Mainland” is too wide. The coverage section names Maple Ridge and Pitt Meadows, the Tri-Cities, and the Fraser Valley, on a map of the cities they actually reach. Someone in Langley can see they are in range. Someone outside it can see they are not.

05

The ask is the job.

The button reads Request Site Deployment. The form asks for the service, the company, and the site. A phone call sits next to it, because a lot of this work still starts that way. Both paths are one action, and the confirmation tells the visitor when to expect a reply: within four business hours.
